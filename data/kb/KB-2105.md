# KB-2105: Aurora AR-900 9.2.1 strict-priority queue can starve best-effort traffic during microbursts

## Summary
On Aurora AR-900 running 9.2.1, a strict-priority egress class can briefly monopolize the transmit scheduler during microbursts on a 10 Gbps uplink. During the affected interval, best-effort traffic may drop to below 1 Mbps for 1-2 seconds even though the interface remains physically up and error-free.

## Symptoms
Operators typically observe short, repeatable latency spikes for nonpriority traffic when the voice class carries sustained microbursts at roughly 40,000-60,000 packets per second. The interface shows no CRC, runts, or link flaps, and general throughput returns to normal after the burst ends.

`show qos interface` indicates that the strict-priority queue is draining at 100% while the weighted queues remain nearly empty or do not advance as expected. In some deployments, the control-plane and L2/L3 forwarding counters remain stable, which can make the issue appear as an intermittent application problem rather than a scheduling defect.

## Diagnosis
1. Run `show qos interface <interface>` on the affected uplink and confirm that the strict-priority queue is at or near 100% drain while one or more weighted queues show little or no service during the same interval.
2. Run `show interface <interface>` and verify that the port is up with no physical errors, CRC increments, or output discards that would indicate a layer-1 fault.
3. Run `show policy-map interface <interface>` and check the voice class packet rate; values in the 40,000-60,000 pps range with a simultaneous starvation of best-effort classes are consistent with this issue.
4. Run `show logging | include SCHED-DEFICIT` and look for `%QOS-5-SCHED-DEFICIT` entries aligned with the latency spike window.
5. If needed, use `debug qos scheduler` for a short capture during the burst and confirm that weighted queue credits are not restored immediately after the strict-priority queue empties.
6. Compare the behavior on a second egress interface or a non-affected release; if the same traffic mix does not starve best-effort traffic, the problem is localized to the 9.2.1 scheduler path.

## Root cause
The scheduler in 9.2.1 fails to reinsert a quantum for weighted queues after the strict-priority queue empties. When a burst fits inside a single scheduling epoch, the priority class can consume the entire transmit window and prevent best-effort queues from receiving service until the next scheduler tick.

The deficit is only cleared on the next 4 ms scheduler tick. That delay is long enough to cause a brief but visible starvation event for latency-sensitive best-effort traffic sharing the same egress port, especially on 10 Gbps links with short, high-rate voice bursts.

## Workaround
1. Limit the strict-priority class so it cannot consume the entire transmit window. The supported mitigation is to cap the voice class to 30% bandwidth on the affected egress policy.
2. If strict-priority semantics are not required, convert the class to low-latency queueing and apply an explicit policer of 300 Mbps on 10 Gbps links.
3. Where traffic design allows, move bulk or non-latency-sensitive flows to a separate egress interface so they do not share the same scheduler with the priority class.
4. After applying the policy change, monitor `show qos interface <interface>` during the next burst and confirm that weighted queues continue to receive service while the priority queue remains bounded.

```text
configure
policy-map wan-egress
class voice
priority percent 30
```

## Resolution
This defect is fixed in release 9.2.3. The scheduler now preserves weighted queue service credits after a priority burst, preventing starvation of best-effort traffic during microbursts.
