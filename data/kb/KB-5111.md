# KB-5111: Atlas SD-WAN 2.9: orchestrator blocks policy push when the CRL update is older than 24 hours

## Summary
On Atlas SD-WAN 2.9 orchestrators, policy pushes can fail when the imported certificate revocation list (CRL) has not been refreshed within 24 hours. The orchestrator stops the push during preflight validation, while existing tunnels remain established and forwarding continues.

This condition affects configuration changes that require a policy push to edges, including routing, security, and application-aware policy updates. The issue is resolved in release 3.1.

## Symptoms
Administrators observe that policy pushes fail consistently once the CRL age exceeds 24 hours, even when the CA has not revoked any certificates. The failure occurs before the job is dispatched to any edge devices, so the orchestrator reports the push as rejected during validation rather than showing edge-side application errors.

Operationally, the overlay remains up. Existing tunnels, routing adjacencies, and active traffic continue to function, but any change that depends on a new policy push is blocked until the CRL is refreshed and re-imported. The problem is most visible on orchestrators that receive CRLs from an external PKI with a publication interval longer than 24 hours.

## Diagnosis
1. Check the orchestrator event log for certificate validation failures during the policy push. Run `show log recent | include CRL` and confirm that the failure is reported before any edge contact, with `%PKI-4-CRL-EXPIRED` in the output.
2. Verify the current CRL age and timestamp. Run `show certificate crl status` and look for a last-updated time older than 24 hours, or a remaining-validity field that has already crossed the 24-hour mark.
3. Confirm that the push is failing in preflight rather than during edge synchronization. Run `show orchestrator jobs detail <job-id>` and check that the job state is `rejected` or `failed-precheck` with a certificate-validation reason.
4. Check that the issue is specific to policy push and not to tunnel health. Run `show sdwan tunnels` on an edge and confirm that tunnels remain `up` while the orchestrator rejects configuration changes.
5. If available, inspect the preflight validation result. Run `show orchestrator validation last-policy-push` and look for a certificate section reporting CRL expiration rather than a revocation hit or transport failure.

Example output:


## Root cause
In Atlas SD-WAN 2.9, the orchestrator preflight validator treats the imported CRL as hard-expired exactly 24 hours after the last update. This logic does not honor the CA-supplied `nextUpdate` field, so the validator rejects policy pushes even when the CRL is still within the CA's intended validity window.

This behavior is most disruptive when the external PKI publishes CRLs on a 48-hour cadence. In that case, the orchestrator blocks pushes for the second half of the publication interval, despite the CRL still being acceptable for revocation checking.

## Workaround
1. Refresh the CRL on the orchestrator before the CRL reaches 24 hours of age. If the current CRL is already older than 24 hours, import a newer CRL immediately so the preflight validator sees a fresh timestamp.
2. If you control the external PKI schedule, reduce the CRL publication interval to 12 to 18 hours so the orchestrator never crosses the fixed hard-expiry threshold between updates.
3. Verify that the updated CRL timestamp is current before retrying the policy push, then re-run the push from the orchestrator after the import is committed.

```text
certificate import crl revocation-list.pem
commit
```

## Resolution
The defect is fixed in release 3.1. In the corrected behavior, the orchestrator validator honors the CRL `nextUpdate` field instead of enforcing a fixed 24-hour timer, allowing policy pushes to proceed until the CRL actually expires.
